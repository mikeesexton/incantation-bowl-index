Clay / Language / MemorySpells that outlived their makers

Protection,
close to home.

A spell against illness. A shield for a family. Written on ordinary clay, these words reveal what people feared—and what they hoped to keep safe.

Originating in Mesopotamia, in present-day Iraq and neighboring Iran, most bowls were made in the sixth and seventh centuries CE, in late antiquity—a world that changed with the rise of Islam.

What we index,
and what we hold back.

An index of who said what about which bowl is useful only if it is honest about its own limits.

A bowl is not a catalogue entry.

The same bowl can appear in several publications under several numbers. The index keeps those appearances separate and records how confident it is that they describe one object, rather than silently merging them.

Every statement has a source.

Dates, findspots, measurements and readings are recorded as claims attributed to a publication, with a page or catalogue locator. Where sources disagree, both readings are kept.

We do not republish other people’s work.

Modern transcriptions, translations, commentary and photographs belong to the scholars, publishers and collections that made them. The index records that they exist and where to find them. It does not reproduce them.

Provenance is reported, not settled.

Many bowls left Iraq and Iran in circumstances that are contested or undocumented. Recording what a source reports about an object’s history is not a statement that its ownership, export or authenticity is established.
