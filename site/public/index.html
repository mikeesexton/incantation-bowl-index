Clay / Language / MemorySpells that outlived their makers

Protection,
close to home.

A spell against illness. A shield for a family. Written on ordinary clay, these words reveal what people feared—and what they hoped to keep safe.

Originating in Mesopotamia, in present-day Iraq and neighboring Iran, most bowls were made in the sixth and seventh centuries CE, in late antiquity—a world that changed with the rise of Islam.

A thousand years later,
the bowls resurface.

1850

A discovery at Nippur

Austen Henry Layard uncovers an inscribed bowl at Nippur. Ancient spells return to view.

1853

The first texts in print

Thomas Ellis publishes bowl texts in Layard’s book. The work of reading their spells begins.

Sources for the story

Brodie and Kersel, “WikiLeaks, Text, and Archaeology,” in Archaeologies of Text (2014), p. 200 dates Layard’s finds at Babylon and Nippur to 1850. Earlier bowls reached the British Museum in 1841.
