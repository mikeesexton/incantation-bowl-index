## Dataset significance

The excerpt shows why a place name alone is an inadequate provenance model. Waller distinguishes:

1. documented or controlled archaeological excavation;
2. museum and accessions-register attributions;
3. dealer and antiquities-market reports;
4. plausible but unverified reports; and
5. bowls with no reliable provenance.

This distinction is now roadmap task `META-005`. The intended result is a second, source-attributed assessment layer. Existing findspot claims will remain intact.

## Source findings

- Only a small minority of bowls have documented archaeological contexts; most surfaced through private collections or the antiquities market.
- The Iraq discussion supplies a useful place-authority seed list and several ancient/modern aliases. It includes documented excavations and separately lists sites reported in museum registers whose provenance could not be verified.
- Susa is identified as the controlled-excavation case for western Iran in this discussion. Other western Iranian findspot reports are treated as unverified.
- The map on printed p. 13 plots documented excavation findspots and intentionally omits unverified cases.
- British Museum 91711 is a concrete warning case. The accessions register attributes it to Layard's excavation at Arban, Syria. Waller calls the attribution plausible but unverified because Layard's account and prospectus do not document the bowl.
