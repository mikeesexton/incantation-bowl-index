# Penn Museum questions to send

Prepared 22 September 2026 for follow-up with Katy Blanchard.

## Scope

The forty main Montgomery/Penn number concordances are already checked. This
short list omits the lower-priority measurement differences and asks only about
catalogue wording, an unusual date, and two language fields.

## 1. B16007 and B16081: a wording clarification, not an identity question

- [B16007](https://collections.penn.museum/collections/object/329808) says
  `Duplicate of16081`.
- [B16081](https://collections.penn.museum/collections/object/316265) is visibly
  a different physical bowl.

This relationship is already clear in the scholarship. Montgomery calls B16007
a “replica” of the previously published B16081, notes that their central designs
differ, and prints their texts in parallel (*Aramaic Incantation Texts from
Nippur*, text 7, pp. 145–149). “Duplicate” therefore appears to mean a duplicate
or parallel **text**, not a duplicate object.

Suggested wording to Penn:

> The B16007 record says “Duplicate of16081.” Montgomery describes B16007 as a
> textual replica of B16081 and compares the two inscriptions in parallel, while
> the photographs clearly show two different bowls. Is “Duplicate of” intended
> here to mean a duplicate inscription? If so, would it be possible to clarify
> that wording in the online record?

## 2. B16086, B16062, B6354, and B6353: what does “Same as” mean here?

- [B16086](https://collections.penn.museum/collections/object/161879) says
  `Same as B16062\B6354`, but its photograph shows a distinct bowl.
- [B16062](https://collections.penn.museum/collections/object/287815) says
  `Same as B16086`.
- [B6354](https://collections.penn.museum/collections/object/68943) shares the
  same reconstructed-object photographs as B16062; the modern photograph is
  labelled `B16062 / B6354`.
- [B6353](https://collections.penn.museum/collections/object/36365) is a
  separately photographed sherd. B6354's description places it in a larger
  fragment group with B6353, B6355–B6359, and B16062.

Modern scholarship also distinguishes the objects: Moriggi treats B16086 as one
bowl and refers separately to B16062 plus fragment B6354 as a Syriac textual
parallel (*A Corpus of Syriac Incantation Bowls*, 2014, pp. 36–37 n. 52).

Suggested wording to Penn:

> The photographs indicate that B16086 and the reconstruction labelled
> B16062/B6354 are different physical bowls, while B6353 is a separate sherd in
> the larger fragment group. Moriggi likewise treats B16062 + B6354 as a textual
> parallel to B16086. In the reciprocal catalogue notes, does “Same as” mean a
> parallel or substantially duplicate inscription rather than the same object?
> And is the intended physical grouping B16062 with B6353–B6359, including
> B6354? I want to preserve Penn's numbering without accidentally merging
> distinct objects.

## 3. B2963: source of the “ca. 200 BCE” date

- [B2963](https://collections.penn.museum/collections/object/40422),
  Montgomery/PBS III text 3, gives `Date Made: ca. 200 BCE`.

The value remains unusual after checking the cited edition. Montgomery discusses
the Nippur bowl group as late pre-Islamic and no later than the sixth or beginning
of the seventh century CE (*Aramaic Incantation Texts from Nippur*, pp. 103–105).
That is group-level context rather than an object-specific redating, but it makes
the catalogue's BCE date worth sourcing.

Suggested wording to Penn:

> B2963 is dated “ca. 200 BCE” in the online record. Could you tell me whether
> that date comes from a particular catalogue or curatorial source, or whether
> it may be a legacy data-entry issue? I would like to cite the basis for Penn's
> value accurately rather than silently replace it with the later dating usually
> given for the Nippur incantation-bowl group.

## 4. B9010 and B9008: two “Hebrew Language” fields

- [B9010](https://collections.penn.museum/collections/object/271621),
  Montgomery/PBS III text 9, gives `Inscription Language: Hebrew Language`.
  Montgomery places texts 1–30 in the Aramaic dialect of the Babylonian Talmud,
  and modern scholarship describes B9010 as Jewish Babylonian Aramaic.
- [B9008](https://collections.penn.museum/collections/object/222626),
  Montgomery/PBS III text 31, also gives `Inscription Language: Hebrew Language`.
  Montgomery explicitly places texts 31–37 in a Syriac dialect, and Moriggi's
  modern corpus edits B9008 as a Syriac bowl written in Manichaean script.

The disagreement is therefore real and not just an inference from Bowlam's
normalization. The likely issue is legacy terminology or a language/script
category, but the online records do not explain it.

Suggested wording to Penn:

> B9010 and B9008 are both labelled “Hebrew Language” in the structured field.
> Published editions identify B9010 as Jewish Babylonian Aramaic and B9008 as
> Syriac in Manichaean script. Does Penn's “Hebrew Language” value reproduce an
> older catalogue category, refer broadly to script, or represent a field that
> should be updated? I would like Bowlam to report Penn's terminology faithfully
> without presenting a legacy label as a current linguistic identification.

## Recommended short agenda

The three questions that genuinely need Penn's input are:

1. What relationship does `Same as` encode for B16086 and the B16062/B6354
   reconstruction, and how does the B6353–B6359 fragment group fit?
2. What is the source of B2963's `ca. 200 BCE` date?
3. What does `Hebrew Language` mean in the B9010 and B9008 records?

The B16007/B16081 point is best framed as an optional wording correction because
the published source already establishes that they are separate bowls with
parallel texts.
