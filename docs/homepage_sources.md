# Bowlam homepage sources

Checked 7 September 2026. These support the introductory narrative and schematic;
they do not change any corpus assertion or object-level findspot claim.

- Dating, protection of households, and geographical extent: University of
  Hamburg CSMC, “What Will Save the Household?” (Artefact of the Month 26),
  https://www.csmc.uni-hamburg.de/publications/aom/026-en.html . Most examples
  are sixth–seventh century, with possible earlier and later examples. Documented
  finds extend from Ashur to the river confluence and into Khuzestan. The map
  shades that broad region schematically, not an exact distribution or boundary.
- 1850: Neil J. Brodie and Morag M. Kersel, “WikiLeaks, Text, and Archaeology:
  The Case of the Schøyen Incantation Bowls,” in Archaeologies of Text (2014),
  p. 200, https://scholars.depaul.edu/ws/portalfiles/portal/39957201/fulltext.pdf .
  Downloaded and extracted the chapter text; the paragraph explicitly dates
  Layard’s Babylon and Nippur discoveries to 1850, citing Layard 1853,
  II:509–526. It also records two bowls acquired by the BM in 1841, so the
  homepage says “a discovery,” not the first known bowl.
- 1853: British Museum W_1841-0726-90_1, bibliographic commentary,
  https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/W_1841-0726-90_1 . Ellis’s
  drawing, transliteration and translation appeared in Layard 1853, no. 6,
  pp. 521–522. Montgomery, Aramaic Incantation Texts from Nippur (1913),
  introduction, also identifies Layard’s volume as the first publication.

No modern scholarly transcription, translation or artifact photograph is reused.
The existing bowl illustration is an artist’s impression.
