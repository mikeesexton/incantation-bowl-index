# Held PDF content extraction audit — 28 September 2026

This audits registered PDF captures against bowl-level structured text and media in the private corpus. A held file or edition pointer alone does not count as extracted content. Counts are source-linked and can include records made from other captures of the same source. They do not certify proofreading or completeness of commentary, indexes, or images. The separate July 2026 literature-review PDF has a `.txt` extraction and is outside the source-capture ledger.

## Extracted in these sessions

- Both complete *Aramaic Bowl Spells* volumes (Shaked, Ford, Bhayro 2013 and 2022): all 119 JBA entries have a private English translation, a working Hebrew-script transcription, and at least one photograph page. Volume one has 90 plate images; volume two has 66 photograph and 23 artist-impression figure page images. Across the volumes, 119 dimensions, 113 client descriptions, and 38 biblical-quotation lists were added as source claims.
- Ford and Morgenstern’s 2020 Jena catalogue: all 68 indexed appearances have private numbered-section text-layer extracts (437,391 characters from the first pass), including descriptions, edited inscription and scholarly translation where printed, commentary, and notes. A page-layout check found nine portions that the PDF emitted before their own entry headings; these are now retained as nine additional source-page supplements. Jena has **36 separately indexed English translations and 35 original-script working transcriptions**, 77 complete-section/supplement rows and 96 retained figure pages. The latest 21 script rows cover 15 further Hebrew-script and all six Syriac/Manichaean edited entries. Entries 4, 16, 21 and 27 do not supply a separately usable English translation here: 4 has magic words and cursive script, 16 prints Hebrew biblical text, and 21/27 lack a meaningful continuous reading. Entry 21 has no continuous original-script edition to separate. The four Mandaic entries (37–40) remain in complete private sections and page facsimiles because the PDF text layer substitutes glyphs; an independently checked Mandaic reading is needed before separate indexing. All split columns remain working readings requiring source-page proofing, especially damaged glyphs, fragment order and line numbers. Entries 41–68 are mostly pseudo-script or uninscribed. The PDF contains entry 69 (HS 3070, printed pp. 243–244), a box of assorted fragments missing from this catalogue’s appearance set; an older Brand-list record exists. Its physical unity and scope require explicit review before an object link.
- Moriggi’s 2014 *Corpus of Syriac Incantation Bowls*: all 49 numbered entries now have a private English translation and Latin-script Syriac transliteration from the two-column edition. Fifty-eight illustrated pages are retained for 46 entries. Nos. 33, 43, and 44 have no illustration in this held volume; no. 33 points to a photograph in Salvesen 1998, while nos. 43–44 list none. The edition text is a working digital extraction and still needs line-by-line page proofing; source descriptions, commentary, and indexes are open.
- Bhayro, Ford, Levene, and Saar’s 2018 Berlin catalogue: all 169 numbered catalogue entries are retained as 170 private source-appearance extracts (entry 168 represents at least two bowls), with 973 additional short source-attributed factual claims. The 16 selected edition sections are retained in full on 17 appearances; 15 contain separately indexed working English translation and Latin-script transliteration blocks. Eighteen private page images cover 17 appearances. Edition XVI is pseudo-script and has no translation. Some selected sections cover fragments from more than one inventory number within one catalogue entry. The generic `summary` text type stores each full protected catalogue or edition extract because the current schema lacks dedicated types; their locators and notes identify them precisely. These text-layer extracts have not been proofread against every printed page.
- Levene’s 2013 *Jewish Aramaic Curse Texts*: all 30 indexed bowl appearances now have the full private edited section plus separately indexed working English translation and Hebrew-script transcription. Thirty-seven retained photograph-page links cover the 14 newly edited appearances; the 16 previously published-text sections contain readings but no new bowl plates in this volume. Twenty-seven concise source claims record dimensions or collection location. The two bowls VA.2496 and VA.2575 are printed in one comparative section; each appearance retains that joint section and reading block with a locator, rather than falsely assigning individual lines. The text layer interleaves Hebrew and English, so the automatic split, Hebrew character order, and damaged characters need page checking; the full source section is retained beside each split.
- Pognon’s 1898–99 Khouabir volume: a direct page check found a separately headed **no. 31** on printed p. 88, absent from the earlier 30-entry inventory. It is now a separate candidate with a printed-page locator; no identity concordance was inferred. All 31 numbered sections have private working OCR extracts, and their French translation spans are now separately indexed as 31 private working translation rows (36,184 OCR characters), with exact printed/PDF page ranges and the retained facsimiles for proofing. OCR footnotes, hyphenation and spacing remain imperfect; these are not checked reading texts. There are 106 linked source-page facsimiles, 30 privately rendered numbered bowl plates, and 29 source-reported plate diameters. No. 30 is an unmeasured fragment plate; no. 31 has no corresponding numbered plate. The scan's Mandaic glyph encoding is corrupt, so no text-layer output is classified as a trustworthy Mandaic transcription.
- Brand's 2021 *Working List of Syriac “Manichaean” Incantation Bowls*: all 41 numbered references and three separately indexed contextual comparisons now have exact, page-linked private list excerpts. This three-page source is a dated bibliographic concordance; it contains no incantation edition or bowl image. The contextual mentions of CBS 16014, MS 2055/16, and MS 2055/25 are labelled as such, without making them additional numbered Syriac-script bowls.

AS 13 has its bowl and exterior detail photographs (pp. 250–252); Davidovitz 27 its interior and exterior views (pp. 257–258); Museo Sefardí 1073 its two credited photographs (pp. 259–260). - Ellis's bowl section in Layard 1853: all six individually numbered bowls now have bounded, searchable full-section working OCR (six rows) plus 16 source-page facsimile links from printed pp. 512–522. Five separate English translation OCR spans are indexed for nos. 1, 2, 4, 5 and 6; no. 3 is presented by Ellis as an abridgment of no. 2 and lacks a separate translation. The Internet Archive OCR companion and held scan are independently hash-bound. - Schwab's 1891 Susiana bowls N–P: each of the three has a bounded full-section French working OCR row and exact page facsimiles (eight links from printed pp. 590–593). N also has a separate French translation OCR row, explicitly qualified by Schwab's uncertain reading; O and P contain descriptive interpretation but no continuous separate translation. The source's Hebrew-script OCR is corrupt. The Internet Archive OCR companion and held scan are hash-bound, and the facsimiles remain available for proofing. No public text or image release was decided.
- Gordon's 1934 Istanbul/Baghdad article: all six edited texts A–F have private source-page OCR, a separately indexed working English translation span, and their individual plates X–XV. E and F share Gordon's combined parallel translation; it is linked to each appearance with that limitation explicit. All seven additional survey-number appearances have their exact survey page OCR and facsimile. In all, 13 appearances gained 19 private text rows and 31 page links. The scanned Aramaic glyph OCR is damaged, so it is not classified as a transcription.
- Gordon's 1937 Aramaic/Mandaic article: all eight edited texts H–O have source-page OCR and separately indexed working English translation spans; the introductory National Museum no. 207962 mention has its footnote page. H–N have their retained edition plates, while O has no usable plate in this assembled scan: PDF p. 36 is blank. Nine appearances gained 17 private text rows and 38 page links. The scanned original-script OCR is damaged and requires image-based recovery rather than classification as a transcription.
- Gordon's *An Aramaic Exorcism*: text G now has its full private working article OCR (printed pp. 466–474), a separately indexed working English translation from pp. 470–471, and all four article plates XXII–XXV. Thirteen exact source pages are privately linked. The original-script OCR is damaged and is not classified as a transcription; the translation requires page proofing.
- Waller's state-of-the-art chapter: its BM 91711 appearance has the complete printed p. 13 discussion and footnote in private text, plus the exact source-page image. The reported Arban, Syria, location is retained as an uncertain, attributed accession-register claim because Waller notes that Layard did not record a bowl find there. This chapter is contextual, not an edition of BM 91711.
- Oriental Institute *Highlights* no. 27: OIM A32675 has its printed p. 51 photograph and entry available privately, a project-authored factual summary, and five source-attributed claims for reported surface recovery at Nippur, catalogue date, dimensions, and pseudoscript. The PDF permits printing but marks text copying as disabled, so its prose text layer was not extracted. The entry offers no inscription edition or translation; the bowl image and legally permissible facts were retained without assigning the catalogue's wording to a text row.

These born-digital text-layer extractions have not had page-by-page scholarly proofing. Damaged lines, word spacing, restoration marks, typography, commentary, linguistic notes, introductory material, and indexes still need extraction or review.
