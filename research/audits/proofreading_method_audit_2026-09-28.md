# Six-source edition extraction audit — 28 September 2026

**Scope.** I screened 36 private edition rows in 18 deliberately spread entries (first, middle, and late entry in each of six large sources). Each entry includes a translation and an original-language row. The [machine-readable sample](proofreading_method_screen_2026-09-28.json) records the exact row IDs, locators, PDF page spans, source SHA-256, row hashes, and text-layer comparison counts without committing protected text. I also inspected rendered PDF pages for ABS1 JBA 1 (PDF p. 84), ABS2 JBA 65 (p. 39), Moriggi bowl 25 (p. 143), Jena entry 20 (p. 126), Levene M102 (p. 122), and Burberry ACB 1 and 24 (pp. 112 and 339–340): eight pages in all.

The numeric screen removes punctuation, spaces, and combining marks, then asks whether each consecutive 12-character window in a stored row occurs on the cited PDF pages. It tests page association and large-scale loss, not faithful wording. Right-to-left PDF extraction, footnotes, editorial symbols, and short fragmentary lines can make the score misleading. Screening did not itself confer `reading_text_checked` status. I subsequently checked Burberry ACB 24's short, nine-line English translation against both rendered pages and entered **that one row** in the proofreading ledger; its existing normalized wording matched the print. Its Hebrew row remains unreviewed.
