## Corrected finding

“Montgomery gives no separate translation for No. 27” is source-specific. It must
not be expanded to “no translation is available.” Montgomery 1913 printed p. 212
(PDF p. 218; held capture CAP-560EB584740F) prints lines 1–11 in Hebrew script and
refers the reader to the close parallel No. 2. He omits damaged later lines. The
facsimile was visually inspected again; its introductory English gloss is not a
complete translation. This is Aramaic in Hebrew script, not modern Hebrew.

The later source already existed as SRC-CA4B122C5F4D, but its only recorded
appearance described NLI 9467.163. No full source capture was present.

## Actual extraction coverage

Read-only queries found 70 Montgomery text rows: 35 translations and 35 summaries,
with **zero transcription or transliteration rows**. TEXT-002 completed checking
those 35 available printed translations; TEXT-003 remains queued for the original
language. The AIT27 source-page image retained on the previous turn is a facsimile,
not a searchable original-script transcription. Claims that all held incantation
texts have been extracted are unsupported.

Other sources already have original-language rows. Before this session’s one
new own-prose summary, the corpus held 226 transcription and 65 transliteration
rows. Those are row counts, not unique-bowl completion. Existing source audits
identify unresolved image-only and broken-font cases, notably Pognon’s Mandaic
texts and four Mandaic Jena entries. Having a PDF, linked edition, checked English
translation or summary never proves original-language capture.

## Feasible next work

1. Capture the lawful source and preserve its exact hash and attribution.
2. Extract each printed original-script column separately, using a good PDF text
   layer where available and manual/image recognition where it is not. Retain
   original line numbers, restorations, uncertainty marks and page facsimiles.
3. Check letter order, words, editorial symbols and column/witness assignment
   against the page. Mark working extraction separately from scan checking and
   specialist interpretation. OCR capability is not an accuracy certification.
4. Index the scholar’s existing translation before producing a new one. For
   this comparative edition, retain the common translation separately and map
   only explicitly supported AIT27 variants; never silently manufacture a
   standalone translation or substitute a parallel’s names.
5. Where no published translation can be located, a Bowlam working translation
   is realistic from a checked transcription. Label it explicitly as AI-assisted
   project work, preserve gaps, distinguish literal rendering from explanation,
   retain alternative readings, cite the base edition and reviewer, and keep it
   private pending specialist review. Reading damaged bowl photographs from
   scratch is a separate, harder task. Fluency alone cannot validate a translation.
