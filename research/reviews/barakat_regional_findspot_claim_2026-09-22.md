# Barakat regional-findspot claim audit — 22 September 2026

## Question and finding

Barakat stock LO.769 says that, beyond Mesopotamia, incantation bowls have been
discovered in Jordan, Syria, Lebanon, Egypt and Uzbekistan. The claim should not
be treated as established in that form. It is class-level catalogue copy, not
provenance for LO.769, and the evidence located does not support all five places
as archaeological bowl findspots.

The closest wording located is the Schøyen Collection's *Magical Literature*
introduction. It says bowls are reported from parts of Jordan, Lebanon, Syria
and Turkey; separately says **an** incantation bowl was found in Egypt; and says
an **inscribed jug**, not a bowl, was found in Uzbekistan. That section supplies
no object identifiers or excavation references. Barakat's wording appears to
have collapsed these different object types into a plural bowl claim.

## Evidence by country

| Place | Assessment | Evidence located |
|---|---|---|
| Syria | Plausible reported bowl, but not a secure excavated context | Waller 2025, printed p. 13, discusses BM 91711, whose museum register says Layard excavated it at Arban. Waller calls the attribution plausible but unverified because Layard did not record a bowl there and the relevant prospectus does not mention one. |
| Jordan | A related inscribed vessel, not a bowl | Waller 2025, printed p. 13 and n. 50, points to an Aramaic execration text from Aqaba/Ayla on a juglet. The excavation literature likewise identifies the object as a juglet and compares it with incantation bowls. |
| Lebanon | No documented archaeological bowl find located | Schøyen makes the regional assertion without an object or citation. Lebanese museum holdings and publications show custody or study in Lebanon, not discovery there. |
| Egypt | One unsupported secondary assertion located | Schøyen says one bowl was found in Egypt but gives no object number, site or excavation publication in the statement. No corroborating object-level source was located in this review. Yale's “Echoes of Egypt” bowl page concerns Egyptian visual parallels and Mesopotamian bowls, not an Egyptian findspot. |
| Uzbekistan | The located source says “jug,” not “bowl” | Schøyen explicitly describes an inscribed jug from Uzbekistan. No scholarly attestation for an excavated incantation bowl in Uzbekistan was located. |

For comparison, Waller's excavation-site synthesis says that all but a handful
of the several hundred bowls with documented excavation associations come from
Iraq (printed p. 10), and that Susa in western Iran supplies the controlled-
excavation bowl evidence outside Iraq (printed p. 12). A 2023 review of Christa
Müller-Kessler's broader survey describes mapped findspots in Iraq, Iran and
Syria; it does not add Jordan, Lebanon, Egypt or Uzbekistan.

## Recommended query to Barakat

Ask for an object number, findspot, excavation or accession record, and
publication citation for each of the five countries. Ask specifically whether
“Uzbekistan” refers to the inscribed jug mentioned by Schøyen, and which object
supports the Egyptian claim. Treat museum location, dealer provenance and
archaeological findspot as separate assertions.
